01

Why the format matters

In accounting, attention to detail is the job. Consistent formatting is the first proof you have it.

Grad students: it's required

Leventhal Graduate Career Services requires its resume format for all recruiting activities.

It's read in seconds

Leventhal's guide notes resumes are often scanned in 6 to 10 seconds. The layout has to work at a glance.

Details are the test

A stray period or mismatched date looks small, but it's the same kind of mistake an auditor is paid to catch.

02

The format, rule by rule

From Leventhal's resume guidelines. Most rules apply to both undergrad and grad resumes; the table after the cards shows where they differ.

Page setup

  • One page, no exceptions, on letter-size paper.
  • Times New Roman or another professional font, the same throughout.
  • 10 to 12 point. The guide says larger is better.
  • Margins: top and bottom 0.5" to 1"; left and right equal, 0.75" to 1".

Header

  • Name and contact details centered.
  • Nickname or English name in parentheses: Jordan (Jo) Trojan.
  • Grad guide: a 1.5 pt line under your name.
  • Contact line in 10 point, with round bullets between items.

Headings and details

  • Organizations and schools in bold.
  • Degrees and position titles in italics.
  • City and state after every school and employer; abbreviate states except in bullets.
  • Dates right-aligned, most recent experience first.

Bullets

  • Solid round bullets, lined up.
  • Start with an action verb: present tense for a current role, past tense for a past one.
  • No periods at the end.
  • Spell out numbers under 10, unless the digit has more impact.

Skills

  • Small round bullets, with subheadings followed by a colon.
  • Certifications: any parts of the CPA exam you've passed.
  • Technical skills, and languages with your fluency level.

Before you send it

  • Proofread every time, for typos and consistency.
  • After saving as a PDF or uploading to a firm's site, open it and check it looks right.
  • Use words the reader understands, not internal jargon.

04

Same job, different resume

Audit, tax, advisory, and industry roles all want accounting students, but they read your experience differently.

The facts stay fixed

Employer, title, and dates never change between versions. Only the bullets do.

Lead with what they want

Read the posting, then put the true details that match it first, in its words.

Keep a master resume

Write every bullet you could use in one long file. Each application is a cut of it.

Open an experience, then flip between the four versions. Highlighted words are the ones that changed to fit each path.

Which version would you send?

Read each posting, then pick the version that fits it.

Assurance Intern: "Test internal controls, vouch transactions to source documents, and document your work in audit workpapers."

Controls, vouching, workpapers. The audit versions lead with testing and documenting.

Tax Intern: "Prepare individual and partnership returns, research tax questions, and explain filing requirements to clients."

Returns, research, and client explanations: that's the tax version.

Corporate Accounting Intern: "Support the month-end close, post journal entries, and prepare budget-to-actual variance reports."

Close, entries, and variance reports happen inside a company. Send the industry version.

06

Detail drills

Six quick calls on the small rules that reviewers notice first. Each option is shown in resume type.

Which date range follows the grad guide?

Short month names and four-digit years. Undergrads following the accounting sample write full months, so pick one style and use it everywhere.

Which bullet follows Leventhal's rules?

Action verb first, the number under 10 spelled out, and no period at the end.

You still work at the firm. Which verb starts the bullet?

Present tense for a current role, past tense once you've left.

Which employer line is formatted correctly for a grad resume?

Organization in bold, then city and an abbreviated state. Italics are for degrees and titles.

You've passed two parts of the CPA exam. Where does that go?

The guide puts completed CPA exam parts in Skills: Passed two parts of the CPA exam: AUD and FAR.

How should your degree appear?

The exact degree name, in italics. No abbreviations.

07

Write bullets the Leventhal way

Leventhal teaches STAR: the situation or task, the action you took, and the result. On the page, that becomes one tight line that starts with the action.

Most important information first

The guide's test: imagine the reader only reads the first half of each bullet. Put the action and the scale up front.

BeforeWas asked by my manager to help with the year-end inventory count at two warehouses.
AfterLed year-end inventory counts at two warehouses, resolving $18K in variances before the audit
ActionSituation and scaleResult

Quantify, or give scope

Did revenue rise, costs fall, or time get saved? Say by how much. If you can't put a number on it, say how big the task was.

BeforeHelped clients with their taxes
AfterPrepared 60 federal and state returns for low-income families, claiming $45K in refundable credits
